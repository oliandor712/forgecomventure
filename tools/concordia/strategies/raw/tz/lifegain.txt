Lifegain: The Commander Archetype, Explained
Gaining life does not win games. Lifegain decks are built on the cards that change that: the ones where every point gained is also a point drained, a card drawn, or a counter placed.

Lifegain: Built around gaining life and the payoffs that turn it into a win condition.
What Lifegain Decks Actually Do
A lifegain deck gains life in volume and runs payoffs that convert it into something else. On its own a high life total only postpones losing, so the archetype is really about the second half: drain effects that mirror your gain onto opponents, draw engines that pay out per life gained, and creatures that grow with the total.
In Commander the archetype benefits from starting at forty, which makes life a spendable resource rather than a cushion. Cards that cost life become close to free, and the deck can afford to pay for effects that a twenty-life format could not support. The strongest builds pair a lifegain engine with a drain payoff so that the same trigger stabilises you and kills three opponents at once.
The Lifegain engine
Three steps that feed each other, which is what makes it a deck rather than a pile of cards sharing a theme.
1 Gain life Small amounts, often, from cards that were doing something else.
2 The payoff triggers Archangel of Thune grows the whole board every time you do.
3 It becomes damage Vito turns the life you gained into life they lose.
↻ Life stops being a buffer and starts being the win condition.
Which Colors Fit Lifegain
White and black are the archetype's colors, and their split is clean: white gains life, black converts it into damage. Orzhov covers the great majority of decks. Green offers large single lifegain effects and creatures that grow with the total, and red is essentially absent. It is the color least interested in gaining life at all.
White The gain half. Repeatable small triggers, mass lifegain, and the creatures that reward a high total.
Black The payoff half. Drain effects, the life-as-a-resource cards, and the combos that convert lifegain into a win.
Green Large one-shot lifegain and creatures that grow with the amount gained, usually in a counters-adjacent shell.
Blue A minor partner, contributing draw engines that trigger on lifegain rather than any gain of its own.

Top 10 Lifegain Cards
The cards that define the archetype rather than the ones winning this week. If you are
building lifegain for the first time, these are the effects to
understand before anything else.
Exquisite Blood ↗
Gain life whenever an opponent loses it. Half of the format's best-known two-card infinite combo.
Sanguine Bond ↗
The other half: opponents lose life whenever you gain it. Together they loop until the table is at zero.
Vito, Thorn of the Dusk Rose ↗
A cheaper Sanguine Bond on a creature, which makes the combo far easier to assemble and tutor for.
Aetherflux Reservoir ↗
Gains escalating life per spell and pays fifty life to deal fifty damage. A win condition that needs no combat.
Archangel of Thune ↗
Puts a counter on every creature you control whenever you gain life, converting incidental gain into a lethal board.
Well of Lost Dreams ↗
Draw cards for life gained, up to the amount you paid. Turns the archetype's dead resource into cards.
Serra Ascendant ↗
A one-drop 6/6 flying lifelink at thirty or more life, which in a forty-life format is almost immediately.
Soul Warden ↗
A life for every creature entering, from anyone. Cheap, relentless, and the trigger most payoffs are counting.
Rhox Faithmender ↗
Doubles all life you gain, which doubles every drain, draw, and counter your payoffs produce.
Felidar Sovereign ↗
Wins the game outright at forty or more life on your upkeep. The most direct payoff the archetype has.
Top 10 Lifegain Commanders
Ten command zones that support the strategy, spread across the color combinations it lives
in. The color identity shown is what each commander lets you play in the other 99.
Commander Spotlight
Oloro, Ageless Ascetic ↗
Gains two life every upkeep even from the command zone, which makes him the format's most passive engine.
Karlov of the Ghost Council ↗
Two counters per lifegain trigger and exiles creatures by spending them. Grows absurdly fast.
Ayli, Eternal Pilgrim ↗
Sacrifices creatures for life, then exiles any permanent once you are ten life ahead. Lifegain as removal.
Licia, Sanguine Tribune ↗
Costs less the more life you have gained and converts a chunk of your total into permanent power.
Willowdusk, Essence Seer ↗
Moves life gained onto a creature as counters, or turns it into damage. The green-black bridge to counters.
Trelasarra, Moon Dancer ↗
Scry and a counter on every lifegain trigger, on a two-drop. Cheap, fast, and consistent.
Lathiel, the Bounteous Dawn ↗
Distributes counters equal to the life gained that turn across your creatures at end step.
Liesa, Shroud of Dusk ↗
Drains every opponent whenever a creature dies and taxes their spells with life. Two payoffs on one body.
Obzedat, Ghost Council ↗
Drains two every turn and exiles himself to dodge removal, returning to drain again.
Heliod, Sun-Crowned ↗
Counters on lifegain and grants lifelink, which makes him both an engine and half of a famous combo.

What to Watch Out For
A high life total is not a win, and it can be an active liability: being at eighty makes you look untouchable, and tables respond by attacking the person they cannot ignore. Make sure the deck converts life into damage, cards, or counters rather than just accumulating it. Watch out too for how quickly the Exquisite Blood combo ends a game; if your pod is not expecting an infinite, say so before you sit down.
Frequently Asked Questions
How do lifegain decks win in Commander?
Through payoffs, not through the life total itself. Drain effects mirror your lifegain onto opponents, cards like Well of Lost Dreams convert it into draws, Archangel of Thune converts it into counters, and Felidar Sovereign or Aetherflux Reservoir win outright at a high enough total.
What colors are lifegain decks in MTG?
White and black. White supplies the lifegain triggers and mass gain, black converts life into damage through drain effects and combos. Orzhov is the standard pairing, with green appearing in counters-focused builds.
Is gaining life good in Commander?
Only when it does something else at the same time. Starting at forty with three opponents means raw life gain rarely changes who wins. It just delays things. Lifegain is strong when each point also drains an opponent, draws a card, or grows a creature.
