Counters: The Commander Archetype, Explained
Counters are permanent in a way that pump spells are not. A deck built on them grows every turn and keeps everything it has gained, even through a wrath, if the right pieces survive.

Counters: Built around +1/+1 counters and the effects that multiply them.
What Counters Decks Actually Do
Counter decks accumulate plus-one-plus-one counters on their creatures and run payoffs that scale with the total. The distinguishing feature is compounding: counter doublers multiply everything placed after them, so a modest board becomes an overwhelming one in a single turn without drawing a single extra card.
The archetype overlaps with almost everything else here. Proliferate ties it to superfriends, token production ties it to go-wide, and the largest creatures in the format are usually counter payoffs. Its own identity comes from the doublers and the counter-manipulation cards: the pieces that do nothing on their own and change the scale of everything else.
The Counters engine
Three steps that feed each other, which is what makes it a deck rather than a pile of cards sharing a theme.
1 Put a counter on it One at a time is fine, because the deck cares about what happens next.
2 Multiply it Hardened Scales and Corpsejack Menace change the arithmetic.
3 Keep them The Ozolith holds everything you built when the creature dies.
↻ Each doubler makes every counter after it worth more.
Which Colors Fit Counters
Green is the primary color by a distance, holding the doublers, the hydras, and most of the payoffs. White adds counter-based anthems and token synergies, blue adds proliferate and evolve, and black adds the minus-one-minus-one counter variant, which uses the same infrastructure aimed at opponents' creatures instead. Red participates least.
Green Doublers, hydras, and the deepest pool of creatures that arrive with counters already on them.
White Counter-based anthems and the token generation that gives the counters somewhere to go.
Blue Proliferate and evolve, which add counters without spending cards on them.
Black The inverse: minus-one-minus-one counters used as removal, running on the same doublers and proliferate.
Red Rarely a core color. Red contributes a handful of counter payoffs and little of the infrastructure.

Top 10 Counters Cards
The cards that define the archetype rather than the ones winning this week. If you are
building counters for the first time, these are the effects to
understand before anything else.
Hardened Scales ↗
One extra counter every time counters are placed. A one-drop that quietly doubles the deck's output all game.
Branching Evolution ↗
Doubles counters placed on your creatures. The cleanest and most reliable multiplier in the archetype.
Corpsejack Menace ↗
The same effect on a 4/4 body, so it doubles counters and pressures the board at the same time.
The Ozolith ↗
Collects counters from creatures that die and moves them to a new creature. Makes removal cost you a body, not your investment.
Kalonian Hydra ↗
Doubles the counters on every creature you control on attack. Two attacks usually ends the game.
Evolution Sage ↗
Proliferates on every land drop, adding counters to every creature and planeswalker for free, every turn.
Master Biomancer ↗
Every creature you play enters with counters equal to its power and becomes a Mutant. Scales the whole deck at once.
Conclave Mentor ↗
An extra counter on placement plus lifegain when it dies. Cheap redundancy for the Hardened Scales effect.
Inspiring Call ↗
Draws for every creature with a counter and makes them indestructible. Card advantage and a wrath answer in one instant.
Simic Ascendancy ↗
Tracks growth counters and wins outright at twenty. An alternate win condition that costs the deck nothing to include.

Commander Spotlight
Ezuri, Claw of Progress ↗
Experience counters that pump a creature every turn, growing permanently across the game.
Animar, Soul of Elements ↗
Grows on every creature cast and reduces their cost, so the deck accelerates as it gets bigger.
Vorel of the Hull Clade ↗
Doubles counters on a permanent every turn, including artifacts and lands with counters on them.
Hamza, Guardian of Arashin ↗
Creatures cost one less per creature with a counter, which makes a developed board fuel enormous turns.
Kathril, Aspect Warper ↗
Distributes counters based on keywords in your graveyard, tying counters to a self-mill plan.
Anafenza, the Foremost ↗
Counters on attack plus graveyard hate stapled on, which shuts down the recursion decks that beat you.
Shalai and Hallar ↗
Turns counter placement into direct damage to every opponent, so growth is also a clock.
Renata, Called to the Hunt ↗
Every creature enters with an extra counter. Mono-green, cheap, and it works with everything.
Roalesk, Apex Hybrid ↗
Arrives with counters and proliferates twice when it dies, so removal still advances your board.
Hapatra, Vizier of Poisons ↗
The minus-one-minus-one side: she turns counters into Snake tokens and doubles as repeatable removal.

What to Watch Out For
The doublers are the deck and they are all noncreature permanents, so a Vandalblast or a Back to Nature hurts more than a board wipe does. Spread the effect across creatures and enchantments rather than relying on one, and remember that counters do not survive a creature dying unless you have The Ozolith or similar. The archetype looks resilient but is only as durable as the bodies carrying it.
Frequently Asked Questions
What is a +1/+1 counters deck in Commander?
It is a deck that accumulates plus-one-plus-one counters on its creatures and plays payoffs that scale with the total. Counter doublers like Branching Evolution multiply everything placed afterwards, so the board grows faster the longer the deck runs.
What colors are best for counters?
Green first, for the doublers, hydras, and most payoffs. White adds anthems and tokens, blue adds proliferate and evolve. Simic and Selesnya are the most common pairings, with Abzan popular for combining tokens, counters, and recursion.
Do +1/+1 counters stay if a creature leaves the battlefield?
No. A creature that leaves the battlefield returns as a new object with no counters, so all the accumulated growth is lost. Cards like The Ozolith and Skullbriar, the Walking Grave exist specifically to preserve counters through that, which is why they are archetype staples.
