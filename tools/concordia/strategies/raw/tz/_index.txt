The 20 Commander Deck Archetypes
Every Commander deck is trying to do something. These are the twenty things: what each archetype does, which colors suit it, and the cards and commanders that define it.
Track your collections, build decks and remember games in the free TurnZero HQ app.
App Store     Google Play
An archetype is a deck's primary game plan: not what is in it, but what it is trying to do
on the turn it wins. Two decks can share sixty cards and be entirely different archetypes,
and knowing which one you have built tells you what to cut, what to add, and which pods you
are a good fit for.
These are the twenty archetypes TurnZero HQ classifies decks into. Most real decks are a
blend of two or three: aristocrats decks go wide, lands decks ramp, and a combo lurks in
the back of plenty of otherwise fair decks. Pick the one that describes the win, and treat
the rest as support.
All 20 Archetypes
Spellslinger   Wins on a pile of instants and sorceries rather than a board of creatures.   Graveyard recursion   Cards come back from the graveyard, treating it as a second hand.   Mill   Empties libraries instead of attacking, winning when opponents cannot draw.   Voltron   One creature, usually the commander, carries the whole game plan.   Go wide   Floods the board with tokens and wins with numbers.   Aristocrats   Sacrifices its own creatures for value and drains the table.   Enchantress   Built around a critical mass of enchantments that draw cards as they resolve.   Artifacts   Built around a critical mass of artifacts and the payoffs that count them.   Tribal   Rewards playing many creatures that share a creature type.   Stax   Taxes or locks down what every player at the table is allowed to do.   Combo   Aims to assemble a specific card combination that ends the game.   Control   Answers everything and wins late, once the table has run out of threats.   Ramp   Accelerates into a mana advantage and casts bigger things sooner.   Aggro   Cheap, aggressive creatures apply pressure early and often.   Group hug   Gives the whole table resources, and wins from the goodwill it creates.   Superfriends   Built around a critical mass of planeswalkers.   Lands matter   Landfall and land-recursion turn the mana base itself into the engine.   Counters   Built around +1/+1 counters and the effects that multiply them.   Blink   Repeatedly exiles and returns its own creatures for their enter triggers.   Lifegain   Built around gaining life and the payoffs that turn it into a win condition.
Frequently Asked Questions
What are the main Commander deck archetypes?
The twenty archetypes TurnZero HQ tracks are Spellslinger, Graveyard recursion, Mill, Voltron, Go wide, Aristocrats, Enchantress, Artifacts, Tribal, Stax, Combo, Control, Ramp, Aggro, Group hug, Superfriends, Lands matter, Counters, Blink, and Lifegain. Most real decks combine two or three of them.
Can a Commander deck be more than one archetype?
Almost every deck is. Aristocrats decks are usually also go-wide, lands-matter decks are usually also ramp, and combo turns up as a backup plan inside decks built around something else entirely. The archetype is a description of the primary game plan, not an exclusive label.
How do I know which archetype my deck is?
Ask what the deck is doing on the turn it wins. A deck that draws twenty cards and then attacks with one enormous creature is Voltron, not card draw. The archetype is whatever the rest of the deck is in service of.
