Tribal: The Commander Archetype, Explained
Tribal is the archetype most people build first, because it starts from a question everyone can answer: which creature type do you actually like?

Tribal: Rewards playing many creatures that share a creature type.
What Tribal Decks Actually Do
A tribal deck (increasingly called typal in official Wizards language, though almost nobody says that at a table) is built around creatures sharing a type, plus lords and payoffs that reward the concentration. Elves, Goblins, Zombies, Vampires, Dragons, Slivers, Merfolk, Angels, and dozens more all support real decks.
The strength of the archetype is that its payoffs stack multiplicatively. One lord giving plus-one-plus-one is modest; three lords is a board of creatures that are each three points bigger, and it happened without any single card doing anything spectacular. The weakness is the mirror image: the deck is a house of cards against a wrath, and against targeted removal aimed at the lords rather than the bodies.
The Tribal engine
Three steps that build to a finish. This one ends the game rather than coming back round.
1 Commit to the type Cavern of Souls means the important ones cannot be countered.
2 The lords pump Vanquisher's Banner adds a card as well as the stats.
3 Attack as one Every creature is bigger because the last one arrived.
The last step wins the game, so there is nothing to come back round for.
Which Colors Fit Tribal
Uniquely on this list, tribal has no home colors: the creature type picks them. Elves are green, Goblins red, Merfolk blue, Zombies blue-black, Vampires black, Angels white, Dinosaurs Naya, Faeries Dimir, and Slivers and Dragons sprawl across all five. Choose the type first and the colors follow automatically, which is why this is the friendliest archetype for a first deck.
White Angels, Humans, Knights, and Soldiers. The color of small creatures made large by anthems.
Blue Merfolk, Faeries, Wizards, and Pirates: evasive types that win through unblockability rather than size.
Black Zombies, Vampires, and Skeletons. The types that get better when they die and come back.
Red Goblins, Dragons, and Dwarves. Fast, aggressive, and by far the best at making expendable copies of themselves.
Green Elves, Beasts, and Dinosaurs. The types that ramp into each other or simply outsize everything.

Top 10 Tribal Cards
The cards that define the archetype rather than the ones winning this week. If you are
building tribal for the first time, these are the effects to
understand before anything else.
Cavern of Souls ↗
A land that makes your chosen type uncounterable. Expensive, but no other card protects a tribal deck's key spells this cleanly.
Path of Ancestry ↗
The budget-friendly version of the same idea, adding a scry whenever it pays for a creature of your type.
Vanquisher's Banner ↗
An anthem that also draws a card for every creature of your type you cast. Fixes the archetype's card-advantage problem.
Herald's Horn ↗
Cost reduction plus a free card off the top each upkeep when it hits your type. Cheap and consistently excellent.
Kindred Discovery ↗
Draws whenever a creature of your type enters or attacks. The most explosive draw engine available to any tribe.
Coat of Arms ↗
Every creature gets plus-one-plus-one for each other creature sharing a type. Enormous, and symmetrical enough to be dangerous.
Shared Animosity ↗
The one-sided version for attackers only, which usually converts a developed board into lethal on the spot.
Metallic Mimic ↗
A two-drop that names your type and puts a counter on every one that follows. Works in any color.
Urza's Incubator ↗
Two generic mana off every creature spell of your type. The single biggest cost reduction available to tribal decks.
Patriarch's Bidding ↗
Returns every creature of the named types from all graveyards. The archetype's post-wrath reset button.
Top 10 Tribal Commanders
Ten command zones that support the strategy, spread across the color combinations it lives
in. The color identity shown is what each commander lets you play in the other 99.
Commander Spotlight
Edgar Markov ↗
Vampires. Makes a token from the command zone on every Vampire cast, which is unmatched value for a tribal commander.
The Ur-Dragon ↗
Dragons. Cost reduction on Dragons and a free permanent from hand on every Dragon that connects.
Sliver Overlord ↗
Slivers. Tutors any Sliver repeatedly, which makes the deck the most consistent tribal build in the format.
Lathril, Blade of the Elves ↗
Elves. Makes tokens on damage and converts a wide Elf board into a drain that ignores blockers entirely.
Wilhelt, the Rotcleaver ↗
Zombies. Turns every Zombie death into a Decayed token and a card, which is the whole aristocrats overlap in one commander.
Kumena, Tyrant of Orazca ↗
Merfolk. Three abilities that convert a Merfolk board into unblockable damage, cards, and counters.
Gishath, Sun's Avatar ↗
Dinosaurs. Puts Dinosaurs directly from library to battlefield on combat damage, and a single hit often ends the game.
Najeela, the Blade-Blossom ↗
Warriors. Tokens on attack, and with five colors available she takes infinite combat steps.
Muxus, Goblin Grandee ↗
Goblins. Digs six deep and puts every Goblin found onto the battlefield at once.
Kaalia of the Vast ↗
Angels, Demons, and Dragons. Cheats one onto the battlefield attacking, every combat, from turn four.

What to Watch Out For
Tribal decks lose to board wipes harder than anything except go-wide, and the lords make targeted removal unusually effective too. Build in recursion (Patriarch's Bidding is in the archetype's top ten for a reason) and resist filling the last ten slots with weak creatures purely because they share a type. A bad card is still a bad card when it is on-theme.
Frequently Asked Questions
What is a tribal deck in Commander?
A tribal deck is built around creatures that share a creature type, together with lords and payoffs that reward playing many of them. Wizards now officially calls this typal, but tribal remains the term almost everyone uses at the table.
What is the best tribe in Commander?
There is no single answer, but Elves, Goblins, Slivers, Dragons, and Vampires have the deepest card pools and the strongest payoffs. Slivers and Dragons get access to all five colors, while Elves and Goblins win on sheer speed and card count.
What is the difference between tribal and typal?
Nothing mechanically. Wizards changed the official terminology to typal because tribal was already a card type on cards like Bound in Silence. Players almost universally still say tribal, and the two mean the same thing in conversation.
