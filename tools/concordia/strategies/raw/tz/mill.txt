Mill: The Commander Archetype, Explained
A mill deck ignores life totals entirely. It attacks the one resource nobody plays around, the library, and wins when an opponent has to draw from an empty one.

Mill: Empties libraries instead of attacking, winning when opponents cannot draw.
What Mill Decks Actually Do
Mill wins by emptying libraries. A player who is required to draw a card from an empty library loses the game the next time state-based actions are checked, so the deck's whole job is to get three opponents' ninety-nine-card decks to zero before they get your life total there.
Commander makes that mathematically hard on purpose. Three opponents means roughly three hundred cards to remove, not sixty, and every point of mill you aim at one player does nothing to the other two. Successful mill decks answer that in one of two ways: mass mill that hits all opponents at once, or a secondary payoff that turns everyone's full graveyards into a real board presence.
The Mill engine
Three steps that feed each other, which is what makes it a deck rather than a pile of cards sharing a theme.
1 Mill a chunk Glimpse the Unthinkable takes ten cards for two mana.
2 The engine triggers Hedron Crab mills again every time a land hits the battlefield.
3 The library shrinks Nobody has lost yet. They just have fewer cards left.
↻ The loss happens on an empty draw, not when the library empties.
Which Colors Fit Mill
This is the narrowest color footprint of any archetype here. Blue is where mill lives as a mechanic, black is where the payoffs and the removal live, and Dimir covers the overwhelming majority of decks. Green mills, but almost always itself. Red and white barely participate at all.
Blue The color of mill itself. Nearly every mass-mill spell, mill-per-draw enchantment, and library-halving effect is blue.
Black Payoffs and protection. Black turns opponents' graveyards into your creatures, and supplies the removal that buys the turns mill needs.
Green Almost exclusively self-mill for a recursion plan. Green does not attack other people's libraries in any meaningful way.

Top 10 Mill Cards
The cards that define the archetype rather than the ones winning this week. If you are
building mill for the first time, these are the effects to
understand before anything else.
Glimpse the Unthinkable ↗
Ten cards for two mana at sorcery speed. Still the most efficient single-target mill spell in the format.
Maddening Cacophony ↗
Eight to one player, or with kicker a quarter of every opponent's library at once. Built for multiplayer in a way most mill cards are not.
Fractured Sanity ↗
Fourteen cards for three mana, with cycling so it is never a dead draw. The efficiency ceiling for single-target mill.
Traumatize ↗
Halves a library in one card. Enormous on turn five, notably worse later. Mill's timing is the opposite of most decks'.
Hedron Crab ↗
Three cards off every land drop, including on other players' turns with a fetchland. A one-drop that accumulates absurd totals.
Ruin Crab ↗
The functional reprint, and the reason a mill deck can run eight crabs. Redundancy matters more here than raw power.
Psychic Corrosion ↗
Mills every opponent whenever you draw, which turns a normal blue draw engine into a table-wide clock.
Sphinx's Tutelage ↗
The same effect aimed at one opponent, with a repeat clause on matching colors that makes it wildly variable and occasionally lethal.
Altar of Dementia ↗
A free sacrifice outlet that mills for the sacrificed creature's power. The bridge between mill and any token or aristocrats shell.
Mesmeric Orb ↗
Mills everyone for every untap, including yours. Symmetrical, but you are the only player at the table who wants it.
Top 10 Mill Commanders
Ten command zones that support the strategy, spread across the color combinations it lives
in. The color identity shown is what each commander lets you play in the other 99.
Commander Spotlight
Bruvac the Grandiloquent ↗
Doubles all mill you deal to opponents. Turns Traumatize into a full library and is the single biggest payoff in the archetype.
Phenax, God of Deception ↗
Tap any creature to mill for its toughness. Rewards big, defensive bodies that are good at surviving anyway.
Szadek, Lord of Secrets ↗
Doubles his own mill total on each successful hit. A commander-damage clock and a mill clock in the same card.
Mirko Vosk, Mind Drinker ↗
Mills to four lands on connect, which in a typical Commander deck means far more than four cards.
Anowon, the Ruin Thief ↗
Rogues mill on damage and grow as opponents' graveyards fill. The tribal-mill hybrid, and the most consistent build in the archetype.
Circu, Dimir Lobotomist ↗
Exiles what it mills, which pre-empts every graveyard-recursion deck the mill plan would otherwise be feeding.
Umbris, Fear Manifest ↗
Exiles cards from libraries and grows enormous doing it, converting the mill plan into commander damage as a backup.
Lord Xander, the Collector ↗
Discard, mill, and sacrifice triggers stacked on one body. Slow but attacks every resource at once.
Oona, Queen of the Fae ↗
Exiles from a library and makes a flier for each card of the chosen color, a mill effect that builds a board.
Tasha, the Witch Queen ↗
Lets you cast what you mill from opponents, turning three libraries into your own card pool.

What to Watch Out For
Mill is the archetype most likely to make a table turn on you, because milling everyone reads as attacking everyone. It also actively helps graveyard decks: filling a Muldrotha player's yard is doing their work for them. Talk about it in your Rule 0 conversation, and know which pods it is a good fit for before you sit down.
Frequently Asked Questions
Does milling a player make them lose in Commander?
Not immediately. A player with an empty library only loses when they are required to draw a card and cannot, normally at the start of their next draw step. Emptying the library does nothing on its own; the loss happens on the attempted draw.
Is mill viable in Commander?
It is viable but demanding. Three opponents means roughly three times the cards to remove, so decks that succeed either mill all opponents at once with mass effects like Maddening Cacophony, or pair a partial mill plan with a payoff that uses the filled graveyards to win a different way.
What colors is mill in MTG?
Blue and black. Blue owns mill as a mechanic and supplies nearly every mass-mill effect; black supplies the payoffs and removal. Dimir is the standard pairing, and mono-blue is viable thanks to Bruvac the Grandiloquent.
