Go Wide: The Commander Archetype, Explained
Go wide is the opposite bet from Voltron: instead of one creature nobody can kill, twenty creatures nobody can kill all of. The whole deck is a setup for one enormous attack.

Go wide: Floods the board with tokens and wins with numbers.
What Go wide Decks Actually Do
Go-wide decks produce creature tokens in bulk and win by attacking with all of them at once. Individually the tokens are irrelevant: a 1/1 does nothing. The deck's job is to reach a number of bodies where one anthem, one trample effect, or one damage-per-creature trigger converts the board into lethal across the whole table.
Structurally that means three layers: token producers that scale, multipliers that turn every token trigger into two or three, and a finisher that cashes the board in. It is the archetype with the widest gap between its worst board state and its best. Five tokens is nothing; fifteen tokens plus a Craterhoof Behemoth ends the game on the spot.
The Go wide engine
Three steps that build to a finish. This one ends the game rather than coming back round.
1 Make tokens A few every turn, from whatever the deck is doing anyway.
2 Double them Doubling Season and Anointed Procession turn three into six.
3 Attack with all of it Cathars' Crusade means the board grows as it swings.
The last step wins the game, so there is nothing to come back round for.
Which Colors Fit Go wide
White is the best token color in the format by a wide margin, both for making them and for the anthems that make them matter. Green supplies the mass-pump finishers and the token doublers, red brings goblins and damage-per-creature payoffs, and black overlaps heavily with Aristocrats by turning the same tokens into drain triggers. Blue is the odd color out.
White The best token generation and nearly every anthem effect. A go-wide deck without white is choosing a harder path on purpose.
Green Token doublers and the mass-pump-with-trample finishers that turn a wide board into a single lethal attack.
Red Goblins, haste, and damage-per-creature effects that let the board win without attacking at all.
Black Drain payoffs and sacrifice outlets: the overlap with Aristocrats, where the tokens are fuel rather than attackers.

Top 10 Go wide Cards
The cards that define the archetype rather than the ones winning this week. If you are
building go wide for the first time, these are the effects to
understand before anything else.
Doubling Season ↗
Doubles tokens and counters. Expensive, format-defining, and the single most impactful card the archetype can draw.
Anointed Procession ↗
The white half of the effect at four mana, and the reason token decks run several doublers rather than one.
Parallel Lives ↗
The green half. Stacking two doublers quadruples output, which is how a modest token engine becomes a lethal one in a turn.
Cathars' Crusade ↗
A plus-one-plus-one counter on everything for every creature that enters. Grows the whole board geometrically and ends games quickly.
Divine Visitation ↗
Every token becomes a 4/4 Angel with flying and vigilance instead. Turns quantity directly into quality.
Craterhoof Behemoth ↗
Mass pump and trample on a haste body. The archetype's most reliable one-card kill from a developed board.
Intangible Virtue ↗
Two mana for a token-only anthem plus vigilance, which is exactly the rate a deck that makes twelve of them wants.
Skullclamp ↗
Equip a 1/1, it dies, draw two. Converts surplus tokens into a card-draw engine and fixes the archetype's biggest weakness.
Impact Tremors ↗
A damage to each opponent per creature entering. Wins without combat, which matters against decks full of blockers.
Secure the Wastes ↗
An instant-speed X tokens, so it dodges sorcery-speed wraths and can be held until the turn it is lethal.
Top 10 Go wide Commanders
Ten command zones that support the strategy, spread across the color combinations it lives
in. The color identity shown is what each commander lets you play in the other 99.
Commander Spotlight
Krenko, Mob Boss ↗
Doubles your goblin count on every tap. The fastest token accumulation in the format if it survives a rotation.
Adeline, Resplendent Cathar ↗
A Human token per opponent on every attack, and she grows with the board. Aggro and go-wide in the same card.
Rhys the Redeemed ↗
A one-drop that first makes tokens and then copies your entire board. The cleanest scaling in the archetype.
Trostani, Selesnya's Voice ↗
Populate plus lifegain on every creature entering, which buys the turns a wide board needs to set up.
Ghave, Guru of Spores ↗
Converts counters into tokens and back. The bridge between go-wide, counters, and aristocrats, and a combo engine besides.
Jetmir, Nexus of Revels ↗
Escalating buffs at three, six, and nine attackers. A commander that states the archetype's thresholds as text.
Prossh, Skyraider of Kher ↗
Kobolds scaling with the commander tax you have already paid, and a built-in sacrifice outlet.
The Locust God ↗
A hasty flier per card drawn. The blue exception, powered by draw spells rather than by anthems.
Purphoros, God of the Forge ↗
Two damage per creature entering, from an indestructible non-creature body. Wins through blockers and through wraths.
Emmara, Soul of the Accord ↗
A token whenever she taps, including for convoke, so she produces every turn without ever attacking.

What to Watch Out For
One board wipe undoes an hour of work, and go-wide is the archetype wraths are printed to punish. Hold something back, run protection like Heroic Intervention or Boros Charm, and resist the urge to dump your whole hand before you can actually win. The second failure mode is quieter: a wide board with no finisher just sits there, so make sure the deck can convert numbers into damage.
Frequently Asked Questions
What is a go-wide deck in Commander?
A go-wide deck creates many small creature tokens and wins by attacking with all of them at once, usually after playing an anthem, a mass-pump effect, or a payoff that deals damage for each creature. The individual tokens are weak; the board as a whole is the threat.
What is the best color for tokens in Commander?
White. It has the deepest token generation and nearly every anthem effect, and it pairs naturally with green for doublers and mass pump, or with black for sacrifice payoffs. Selesnya and Naya are the most common go-wide color combinations.
How do go-wide decks deal with board wipes?
By not overcommitting, and by running protection. Keeping token producers in hand, playing instant-speed makers like Secure the Wastes, and holding an effect that grants indestructible or hexproof all let a deck rebuild or simply survive the wrath it knows is coming.
